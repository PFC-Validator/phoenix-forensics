1. Stated handover, retained control

On 12 August 2024, Philipp Mager’s TLA launch proposal specified transfer of contract administration to Phoenix Directive Stewardship after one month and configuration ownership after three months. The original document identifies the ERIS administration and configuration wallets.

“Contract Admin will be transferred to PD Stewardship after 1 month.”
“Ownership transferred to PD Stewardship after 3 months.”

Read the terms in the original repository

The reviewed chain records show that the authority remained outside Stewardship and was repeatedly exercised. In his public reply on 19 October 2026, Philipp explained that ERIS continued to develop, maintain and operate the contracts and had not considered transferring authority appropriate because of concerns about the board.

That explanation records his decision to withhold the authority. No replacement authorization for the published transfer terms was located in the reviewed records.

The strict transaction count below uses 27 November 2024 at 06:02:52 UTC, three calendar months after proposal 4817’s voting end, as a conservative common cutoff. The launch text does not specify a start-date formula. “Ownership” here refers to contract configuration ownership; it is not a blanket transfer of all software intellectual property.

5. Milestone approval without cost or outcome accountability

The approvers decide that a milestone is met and authorize payment—including payments to a director who participates in the vote. That approval does not explain why the work should cost the amount requested or what measurable benefit Terra received. The approvals cited below contain no itemized cost basis, independent price comparison or quantified outcome targets. Declaring work complete does not answer any of those questions.

10,000 USDC for analytics

Philipp proposed and voted YES on A215, enabling the Creda analytics milestone. The Treasury paid 10,000 USDC on 27 January 2026. On 18 October 2026, the dedicated analytics page still displayed “Analytics data will be displayed here...” and “Performance charts will be displayed here...” Payment had been released, while that page still showed placeholders almost nine months later.

45,000 USDC for “Project Kickoff”

The approved 125,000-USDC Estima grant was divided into 45,000 USDC for “Project Kickoff,” 45,000 USDC for “Mainnet Launch,” and 35,000 USDC for “Analytics & TLA Integration.” These terms name stages of work; they set no targets for users, revenue or return on the grant. Philipp voted YES on A271, which released the 45,000-USDC kickoff payment. The original grant approval, A266, was passed by the other three directors.

6. A272 would have removed the need for independent consent

A272 sought removal of a member and a threshold reduction from 75% to 60%. With three equal remaining seats, Philipp and MB could approve proposals together even if the third director voted NO. They both voted YES on A272. A272 failed; the captured current configuration remains four equal members and a 75% threshold.

The proposed structure would have made the financially connected pair sufficient to approve decisions without another member’s consent.

The later temporary voting undertaking would limit intended votes, with an exception for onboarding new directors. It would not remove the on-chain ability to authorize actions. The exception also concerns who controls the future board.

The updated Emergency Supervisor proposal allows scrutiny of any director where relevant, but remains framed around the dysfunction underlying A272 and contemplates a removal instruction. The removal process therefore sits alongside the proposed threshold change and the existing distribution of technical and financial powers.

With the present 75% configuration, explicit abstention can affect the denominator; a missing vote is not an abstention. The decisive A272 comparison is two YES votes against the third member’s NO in the proposed three-seat structure.
